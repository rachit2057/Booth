Framing the problem space

Trust in voting isn't digital.
It's physical, social, and ritual.

We broke voting down into its actors, rituals, and points of trust — and found the trust lives in things you can see and touch.

02 — Trust is physical

People trust visible actions

Booths, machines, and indelible ink are the proof. Trust is anchored to things you can witness — not to a backend you can't.

03 — Voting is social

Participation is shared

Voting is seen, talked about, and performed together. It's a community ritual — the inked finger is a badge worn in public, not just a private transaction.

01 — Access gap

Voting depends on location

Single-day, single-place participation excludes everyone living away from their registered home.

Key insights & synthesis

Three findings that reframed the brief.

01

The Ink Paradox

Indelible ink — designed as fraud prevention — became the primary social proof of participation. The medium has overtaken the message.

02

The Digital Trust Deficit

Tech adoption doesn't correlate with trust. EVM skepticism persists across literacy levels — the gap is emotional, not informational.

03

Atmospheric Weight

Queues, heat, crowds shape decision confidence. Physical discomfort gets conflated with institutional friction — and friction reads as legitimacy.

Global benchmarks · digital success

Where digital voting already works — and why.

Estonia
51%
Online voting in the 2023 elections

A trusted e-ID infrastructure built over decades, plus an "override" system: digital votes are editable, and a physical vote always overrides and is final.

Switzerland
~90%
Postal ballot usage in some cantons

Remote voting is a habitual civic behaviour. Routine postal voting normalised voting-from-anywhere long before it ever went digital.

Persona · The Remote Voter

A digitally fluent voter, limited by location.

Age 18–30 · student or young professional · living away from home. Able to use digital systems with ease, but unable to vote because of where they are.

Barriers

Effortful — travel cost & time for single-day participation. Low trust — tied to physical voting, not digital systems.

Motivation

Socially driven — the ink selfie, peer validation. Wants voting to feel meaningful and serious.

Core innovation

The Digital Curtain.

Recreating the sanctity of the polling booth through technology — so every remote vote is free, private, and deliberate. Run the demo.

01

Environment Lockdown

Blocks screen sharing, recording, and incoming calls for the entire ballot. The booth's secrecy, enforced in software.

02

Intentional Friction

A 10-second mandatory delay before the vote can be confirmed — deliberate weight, so the moment feels as serious as it is.

03

Anti-Coercion Override

A one-time re-vote window inside a secure grace period. If you were watched or pressured, you can quietly change your vote.
